GAME HELP
 


    		  
2/12/02, 3:13 PM PST (GMT -08:00)

Bottom line: Server trades are not supported by MCO. We strongly caution players against doing this, to protect themselves from potential scams.

Let me give some background on why things are the way they are.

Like all other online games, MCO has several servers. Each server (also known as a shard) is unique in terms of its players, economy, trades, etc. There are many reasons why online games are divided up into multiple servers. One reason is that building a huge server is much more expensive. Another is that it's much simpler, from a design perspective, to balance a game for 2,000 simultaneous players than it is for 20,000. (Just imagine 5,000 players bidding on the same auction at Muscle Mike's. Wouldn't that be chaotic?) For this reason, assets (money/cars/parts) on one server are for that server only.

Now let's talk about personas. Like many other online games, MCO allows you to play on more than one server. This is done so that players can still access the game in case their primary server is down. Servers are not bullet-proof, and do occasionally go down outside of scheduled maintenance windows, as you may have experienced. Sometimes the problem has to do with our internet service provider. Sometimes it has to do with hardware failure. And sometimes it has to do with software glitches. No matter what the reason, it still has the effect of making you, the player, unable to play! With that in mind, the MCO team decided to design the game so that players can have a persona on more than one server.

Now you have all the background you need to understand how "server trades" came to be. Because the game allows players to trade with each other within a single server, and also allows players to have personas on more than one server, some players have started to figure out ways to "transfer their wealth" between servers. This involves players agreeing to trade on one server and then following up with a reciprocal trade on a second server. For example, Player A gives Player B a '65 Mustang on Crossroads. Later on, both players log onto Uptown, and Player B gives Player A the cash agreed upon for the trade.

There are many problems associated with this. What if Player B refuses to acknowledge that he has received a '65 Mustang from Player A on Crossroads? What if Player B doesn't log onto Uptown? What if Player B uses a different persona name on Uptown, which allows him to hide from Player A easily? What if Player B gives the Mustang to his friend, then deletes his persona on Crossroads so Player A can never find him? The list of potential problems goes on. And what’s worse is that Player A can’t expect much help from the MCO support team, because MCO doesn't support this activity.

Some of you may ask "why not"? The simple answer is: because the servers are not built to communicate with each other. They are designed to function individually, and thus our ability to police between servers is limited. It's also because so-called "server trades" go against the principal game design. With that in mind, know that we will never plan to build a system that endorses such transaction.

So the moral of the story is "if you server-trade, you do so at your own risk". If you enter into one of these cross-shard "deals" and you trade away all your cash and never see the prized car you were trading for on another server, you're out of luck, friends. If you want my personal opinion, stay as far away from it as you can and caution all players that they may be scammed through server trades.

~~ "Trouble"
