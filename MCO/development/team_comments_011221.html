HIGH STAKES
PORSCHE UNLEASHED
 


    		  
12/21/01, 3:07 PM PST (GMT -08:00)

Hey everyone! I hope you enjoy our latest update. Here are some major features that I'd like to comment on.

The new "Righteous Rides" dealership: There has always been much debate about the Motor City Auction structure. In its purest sense, it was meant to be a self-regulating way to release parts and cars into the world, rewarding players who were skilled enough to earn the money necessary to buy these cars and parts. In reality, the auctions ended up catering more to the "veteran" players, or players who spend large amounts of time racing, and left behind the "casual gamer".

In order to alleviate this problem with the parts auction, we placed all parts available in auction in the Parts Catalog as well. Our next step was clearly to do something similar with the higher-end cars. The new "Righteous Rides" cars will be available for purchase to all players of the appropriate level who have the necessary funds. We're aiming to reward all players for taking the time to work up through the levels, in addition to providing everyone with a frustration-free car buying experience if auctions become too heated.

Always remember, it's beneficial to seek out friends in the community to race against to help you level, and clubs will often be willing to front a good racer some cash in return for his times on the track.


The release of new parts and engine blocks to auctions: The world of Motor City will continue to grow over time. You can expect to see new parts added to the world this patch, as well as many future patches. Each time new parts arrive, they will continue to allow players to build different types of engine configurations, upgrade their suspensions, improve their aerodynamics and enhance their acceleration and top speed through transmissions and rear ends.

The way a player gets parts may be a moving target. Some may be in the catalogue, some in auctions, some only on cars you can buy, and others may be special "prize only" parts! In the end, we plan on allowing every player the opportunity to expand their garage, buy or win new parts, and make their dream hot rod jump off the start line reaching higher top speeds than previously possible. Just remember, going 212mph in a Mustang over the farm dirt roads of Skoggit County Road may not be as easy as you'd think!


The new Car Class system: This is a feature that was discussed in beta as a big enhancement to the racing community of Motor City. It's also a feature we have been anxious to get in the game, as we feel it will add a lot of depth and make all our cars equally enjoyable to play with.

The Car Class system will really bring a greater emphasis to the car building aspect of the game. Instead of simply building any car to the most HPs that it can handle, players will now need to tinker in their garage to achieve the "perfect" HP/Weight Ratio. If you want to be at the top of Class C, then you'd better build a car that sits at exactly 0.129 Power to Weight! In addition, owners of light cars may start looking for parts with a lot of weight. (Maybe that heavy traction bar isn't such a bad idea anymore!)

In order to compete in certain Class Restricted Races, players will also need to be able to build and handle some of the vintage cars (like a '32 Coupe) on some difficult street tracks. In summary, Car Classes will give every player an equal chance to win with their car of choice-the days of needing a Mustang to be competitive are gone.


The new Multiplayer Reward structure: Ah, yes, the new multiplayer reward structure. What's this all about?

Simply put, we want players to race each other. Personally, I have the most fun racing other players, making friends, making enemies...errr rivals than I do running circles in a Time Trial. We also wanted to reward those folks who actually set up an eight-lap, night, weather, backwards race on Skoggit County Road! You will notice the amount of money you earn is still dependent on where you place in the race, but there will always be money "added" to the purse in a multiplayer race to offset repair costs. And remember, the point rewards "scale" based on level of players and level of track. So if you want to really see how much you can win, get a few high levels to race each other on a level 20 track!

The rewards structure for any facet of Motor City can always be changed, so this may not be the last economy change you see. If we see that more money needs to be awarded in multiplayer races, then we will do so.


The new set of Sponsored Cars: The set of Sponsored Cars is something we plan on continuing to change from time to time, to continue adding variety to the game. The set of cars for the December patch include the '70 Plymouth AAR Cuda, '63 Chevrolet Stingray, '66 Shelby Cobra 427 S/C, '70 Ford Boss 302, '67 Chevrolet Camaro Z-28, and the '73 Pontiac Firebird TA. Some of you may recognize a few of these cars back from the days of beta. These are some of the most prized cars in Motor City and they're packing quite a bit of horsepower, so make sure you have your seatbelts fastened!


The adjustment of car weights: While designing the game, the problem we always faced with car weights was the fact that the data is somewhat historical. We found in our research that we'd often come up with three or four different weights for one car! So, we had to make choices-- sometimes directly associated with game balance (remember the Nova?), and other times, they were based on source "reliability".

Partially based on many valid arguments we saw from players on the MCO Message Boards, we felt the time was right to do a thorough review of all the car weights, and make some changes. This time we used one source (Standard Catalog of American Cars 3rd Edition-Krause) so that there would be no doubt as to where the numbers were derived from. Most of the cars we adjusted ended up losing weight.


The "allow/disallow NOS" option: First, for any of you who don't know what NOS is: NOS (Nitrous-Oxide System) increases the amount of oxygen going into the combustion chamber of the engine block. When used effectively, it gives your car a huge power boost for straightaway sections of track. You can see how this might be a huge advantage in a drag race.

This was another much-requested feature on the Message Boards, and is based on real drag racing rules. With the introduction of Car Classes, we figured it would be a good time to add this restriction as well. NOS is calculated in the stats of a car, but now we give the player the added power to disallow NOS if they so choose.
