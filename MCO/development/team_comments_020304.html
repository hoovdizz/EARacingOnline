3/4/02, 5:01 PM PST (GMT -08:00)

BigBen from City Center picks Ghoughakka's brain. Read on for details!

BigBen: How long have you been involved with MCO?

Ghoughakka: I've been involved with MCO ever since NFS High Stakes shipped. Although right after NFSHS I was pulled onto different projects here and there. Overall, it's been over three years.


BigBen: What has your role been in the development of MCO?

Ghoughakka: I've been an Assistant Producer. Essentially my role was to help design the features that comprised MCO, make sure they were technically possible, work with the artists on cars and tracks, and make sure everything gets done cleanly, bug free and on time. Specifically, I spent a lot of time tuning the drive of MCO, designing the Club aspects of MCO and setting the reward structure for racing. In reality, everyone on the production team did just about everything at one time or another. We worked more as a team than a bunch of individuals with specific roles.


BigBen: Do you maintain a persona in MCO?

Ghoughakka: I do. I have my regular EA persona which is Ghoughakka, and that persona is available to me on every shard. That way I can check bugs that are reported easily at any time. I also play with my own personal account with another persona on specific shard. I'd rather not reveal that persona's name.


BigBen: What is your favorite car class?

Ghoughakka: I love to race vintage and I love to race the '32 Coupe specifically. We used to have races at work when the physics were a bit different way back even before beta, and we'd all race '32 Coupes with the fenders off and scream around corners on two wheels. It was a blast! So I still retain those memories, and I'm a fan of the Vintage class for sure.


BigBen: What do find yourself driving?

Ghoughakka: I've got a bunch of cars. A '32 Coupe, '40 Coupe, '49 Merc, '57 Bel Air, '57 Fairlane, '65 Stang, '70 Nova. My best car is the Stang and it is just shy of 600hp. I'm generally a fan of Ford cars, although I'm not a true fanatic. My next goal is to build up a Torino, but that should be fairly simple. In the long run, I would love to make a sweet Galaxie. That car has always been difficult for me to get to drive well, so it'll be a long term project I suppose. If I stuff too much power in there, I just can't handle the street tracks. It's a tricky car to find the right balance in.


BigBen: MCO has several features that fall into the "Easter Egg" category, in that users often stumble onto them accidentally. Some of my favorites are the high-low beam headlights, and revving the engine of a car by right clicking on its image. What are your favorite features?

Ghoughakka: Woah, those are Easter Eggs? Since I am an NFS veteran, I thought that the Real Time Reflections were a big step forward. I remember the first time we had it working in the studio I took a car out on Bel Air, and just drove really slowly, under bridges and lights and was just amazed that it was reflecting the world.


BigBen: No game can ever include everything the developers wish. Are there any ideas that "almost" made it into MCO that you are particularly fond of?

Ghoughakka: That's a big list unfortunately. I suppose the main things that I wished were part of the game are Cruise Mode, Co-Op Racing, and User Submissions. Now, with that said, we are still going to work on these things. Cruise Mode would be the most difficult and may mean a whole different game, but Co-Op is slated for next month if all goes well, and User Submitted paint jobs are a work in progress. I think if we had more time, I would have wanted MCO to be a 3D environment where players drove to where they wanted to go, and interacted with each other in a big world. The thing that's interesting about that particular game though would be that you'd build a very complex part of the game that has absolutely nothing to do with racing. With MCO, we made a conscious decision to focus on making a great "racing" game. I just wish we had the time to also make a great 3D environment too. The reality is, just getting racing to work over the internet and all the server code that needs to support that was a major task in itself.

I'll also add that like every other fan of MCO I want to see more cars and more tracks (where's my Cobra dangit!). We will be releasing these over time as they are completed and approved through licensing. I wish we could push out tracks faster, but as many of you know who have played online games before, tracks require a hefty download and we don't want to burden those on a 56K modem with hours of download times for a track very often. I will say this about the next track though.....any old school NFS'ers may recognize it! Oh, and I'll leak this too...it's a high speed circuit track. Hopefully players are currently enjoying the recently released Hazard Hollow Street track.


BigBen: What about MCO are you most proud of?

Ghoughakka: There was a point in beta when I finally had time to actually spend a full day "playing" MCO instead of sticking my nose in spreadsheets all day. I got in the game and immediately started spamming (I know, I'm evil) for multiplayer races. Eventually, I convinced some folks to head out to Hickey's Ridge (cause I knew that track well). I was worried a lot about the game, mainly because I was dealing with all the bugs that were cropping up, but once I got out on the track, I couldn't believe how much fun I was having. Each race came right down to the wire. And we raced eight times, with each player winning twice! It was just so much fun, I never wanted to stop. I convinced the group to head to Bel Air because we wanted to try a Circuit race, and as the host I turned the track to Backwards. That completely leveled the playing field and it was as if we were all racing the course for the fist time. I remember the surprise as I went hurtling into the air after going over the bridge for the first time. I know I shouted something, but I can't really repeat it here. Anyway, I did feel very happy about the game after that day, and I had a great time. In the end, I knew MCO had elements I enjoyed, like meeting other players, and racing against them and having a good time while building up a character. When you're making a game, all you can hope for is that you make a game you enjoy. You always know popular success is determined by many other factors out of your control, but if you can be secure that you made a game you enjoy playing and that you enjoyed making, that's all you can really ask for.


BigBen: MCO has been running long enough now to have developed its own culture and history. Since humans are notoriously difficult to predict, what about the MCO community has been most surprising to the EA staff?

Ghoughakka: There are a few things that were surprising. I was very surprised by how fast certain players leveled. We had Level 30's in the game in a month and I was amazed. Now there are Level 50's and I'm still amazed. In addition, it was interesting to see how passionate players got about the rivalries in the game. There were a few incidents involving specific clubs on the boards where people would just be bashing each other verbally back and forth. I knew people would be excited, but I didn't know they would get so vicious over Club Turf. Enemies were certainly formed during those days. I am also very pleased that players are helping craft the future of the game. It's true that sometimes it's difficult to read the MCO boards because some posts are just filled with shouting, but there are a large number of discussions that happen on the boards that contain extremely well thought out ideas that would enhance the game. We have a very intelligent player base for sure. I also enjoy sneaking into the game as my unknown persona and talking to club leaders and other players, generally pretending to be a "noob" and just see what they have to say about the game. This gets me a true new player view of how they are treated in the game.


BigBen: Open trials and sponsored racing seems to have taken on a greater role in MCO than many users expected. Was this a surprise to the EA team?

Ghoughakka: Yes and no. There is something missing in MCO which is Co-Op racing. Without Co-Op racing, Multi-player racing is simply PvP racing. Now, some people love to PvP, but we also knew that many did not like the extreme competitive nature of PvP. That's why we had Time Trials and Sponsored Racing in the game, to soften the blow for those people. We figured it would be popular, but that Multi-player racing would be equally as popular. As we all found out, that was not the case. Almost everyone stayed in Time Trials because it paid out well, and it did not have any other players who would mess you up in the race. We have since taken steps to make Multi-player racing far more attractive and looking at the latest numbers, it appears many of the players are now finally moving out of the Time Trials and into Multi-player races. I think this is a very good thing because as I stated earlier, I just have a ton of fun when I race against other people. Yes, it sometimes takes putting the ego aside a little bit, but it's well worth when you have a ton of fun racing other players.


BigBen: Do you expect the Feb patch to make a significant change in how people spend their time in MCO?

Ghoughakka: Yes. So far, this is proving true. Where there used to be single digits in the Street Racing column, there are now hundreds of players found there. Lately, I have no trouble finding a Multi-player race, where as in the past, it was almost like pulling teeth sometimes. The new parts we've released via Part Prizes have also spurned a rash of trading. We hope in the future to release more suspension parts and tires so that mechanics in the game will have even more tools to play with when taming those 800hp beasts.


BigBen: With Hazard now released, are there other tracks in design?

Ghoughakka: Yes, yes and more yes! As a matter of fact, one of the track artists sits right behind me. I've raced all four tracks he's been working on, and well, they are fantastic! There will always be new tracks added to MCO over time. Some of them will be street and some of them will be circuit tracks.


BigBen: Care to tease us with any new track details?

Ghoughakka: Bah, putting me on the spot! Well, I've already hinted that old NFS fans will be very happy with some of the tracks in the pipeline. There is also a track that has a similar feel to MC Classic. These will be circuit tracks. As for the unreleased Street Tracks that are complete or near complete...I'll just tell you that one of them is named Industrial, and you may need to swerve out of the way of a Crane at some point. Oh, and watch out for the train, heh. It hurts!


BigBen: How happy have you been with the population of the MCO world?

Ghoughakka: Any game producer would love to see as many people as possible playing the game they made. So it's difficult to judge how "happy" you are. We have over 31,000 subscribers now and that's a lot of people. I can't say I'm sad about it.


BigBen: Are there any new marketing efforts being considered?

Ghoughakka: Yes there are some things under discussion which I'm probably not at liberty to talk about.


BigBen: Adding any cars outside of what came out of Detroit from the pre-eighties seem to elicit strong feelings from users. If MCO were to push the genre envelope, what new cars or car types would be considered?

Ghoughakka: Hmmmm...there is no answer to that question. The current definite plan is to add more MCO cars as we have been over time via patches. As for other types of cars? Well, I can't say. Could be an Asian car invasion. Could be some European super cars. You'll just have to wait and see what happens.


BigBen: What problems or issues does EA have to solve before user created paint jobs can be introduce

Ghoughakka: I can talk generally on this. It simply comes down to three things. Staffing to handle this, technology to get it into the game, and legal issues. The staffing part would have to be a group of people who figure out a way to collect all the submissions in a format that is universal and that can be judged effectively and quickly. The technology part is how to get them onto the car, into the database, and downloaded to every user on the shard so that everyone can see the new paint jobs without causing a large download or breaking the game in any way. If they are sold, they have to be added somehow to, say, the detail shop, and if the player got proceeds from sales, it would take a lot of code to handle that. The legal part is what can and can't be displayed on each paint job, and what would the relationship of the final product be with both the creator and EA. It's not trivial, but we are working on getting this all together so that it gets into the game soon.


BigBen: If user created paint was implemented, would it be open to anyone to submit work, or would it operate on some sort of restricted or "prize" basis?

Ghoughakka: We'd probably have it on a contest basis, although anyone would be allowed to enter. If we could do anything we wanted, I'd love to see user created paint jobs appear in the detail shop and the player who creates them gets 50% of the sale of every paint job.


BigBen: What's next for MCO?

Ghoughakka: We're focusing on bug fixing for the next patch. We want to identify all the outstanding bugs, and issues that have been reported by our players and fix them. We also plan on adding Co-Op mode in March as well. As for long term, it changes a lot so I can't really commit to anything specific.


BigBen: And finally, what has been your favorite "MCO Moment" so far?

Ghoughakka: Again, this was a multi-player race. I was just about to complete a long race on Skoggit County Road, and I was neck and neck for first place with another player. It was a sponsored race, so both our cars were the same. It all came down to the lines we took, and I think we exchanged the lead six or seven times in that last burst through the town area heading to the finish line. I was the first one to hit that little space on the left side between the building, but as I went through it I got tapped in the rear bumper by my competitor. I almost lost it but managed to recover enough to be swerving back and forth towards the finish line. My competitor also started to lose control as a result of my swerving and we basically collide with each other. I flip over, he starts spinning out of control. My cars ROLLS over the finish line just a split second before my spinning buddy does and I win the race! It was by far the most exciting finish I've ever had, and the most satisfying.


~~ BigBen & Ghoughakka
