4/12/02, 12:32 PM PST (GMT -08:00)

Ahhh... Custom paint jobs. Everybody wants them. The question is how to get them into the world.

Many ideas have been tossed back and forth within the community. Let me try to sum them all up and communicate what we can and cannot do currently. Also the issues that we need to consider along the way. But the bottom line is, we will have custom paint jobs. : )

Interactive Paint Shop - The ability to pickup spray paints and just paint the car within the game. Though this is a great concept, it is also one technical feat. If we go down this route, we're pretty much building a 3D painting tool. It has to be robust, or it's meaningless and ineffective. Given other tasks the team could be focusing on, this idea, unfortunately, is too resource heavy.

Custom Select Paint Color - A not-so-robust version of the Interactive Paint Shop concept. The player has the ability to choose color and shade of a base paint job. Sounds simple. But this concept breaks the paradigm of Motor City. How so? Let me try to explain... Essentially, every car is put together by a list of parts including the paint job. Therefore, each paint job is an unique item in the world. If we open the possibility of 'infinite' or 'massive' combination of shades and colors, we're adding lots of parts into the world. That's not a very elegant solution. We'll be better off if we develop a technical solution for this. That puts us back to the same place as Interactive Paint Shop...

Player Created Paint Jobs - This is simple and has been done by many players. Simply edit an existing paint job file (also known as skins) and overwrite the file in Motor City. The player who made this change can see his/her new paint job, however, the rest of the community cannot. We want to elaborate on this concept and make sure all players can see this custom paint job. But we have the following issues to consider:
  • Quality: The quality of the paint job has to be consistent with the Motor City standard. Which means to make a paint job truly worthwhile, it has to look good. Not to mention it has to be bug free. And by that I mean the paint job (skin) is properly applied onto the car. Anyone with experience creating skins know it can be a tricky task to line up the edges of various texture maps.

  • Update Size: To ensure a paint job is visible to all players in the world, the paint job file has to be downloaded to all players. Which means it has to come down through an Update. Because a typical Update comes with other content, we have to be careful not to overload our patcher with too much content. This will limit the number of paint jobs we can introduce.

  • Terms of Service: We have to ensure all paint jobs are TOS compliant. If any of the paint jobs come across offensive, we cannot distribute it. Also, there are legal issues that we need to consider. For example, some of the marks cannot be modified because our agreement with our licensors. Consequently, all the player created paint jobs must go through a screening process. This can be time consuming based on the number of submissions.

Hope I've covered the major ideas of custom paint jobs, let's talk about what the team plans to do. And please keep in mind this is just our thought process. It may change over time. : )

1. Allow Players to Test Skins - In a future update of MCO, we'll provide the ability for players to load up skin files from their local machine into the game. This will essentially 'apply' the skin to the player's active car. Players can then test the skins to make sure it looks right from all angles. Also take some screenshots and get feedback from others.

2. Allow Players to Submit Skins - Very much like the story and event submission. Once a player completes the skin, he/she can submit it to us. We will then do some internal (or external through Test Center) testing of the skin. If it passes TOS, legal, and testing, then it becomes a candidate to be added into the world. If it fails, the submission is rejected.

3. Ask Players to Vote - Assuming we have a lot of candidates, to minimize update size, we will ask the community to vote on them. The top X skins will then get patched down along with the regular update. We may skip this step if the update size is not an issue.

4. Introduce the Skins - Once the skins are patched down to all players, we will introduce the skins in many different ways. Sometimes the skins will become available through the Detail Shop. Sometimes the skins only appear in Auctions. Some might appear in Righteous Rides. The team will decide how the skins are released.

5. Reward the Players - Now let's talk about rewards. If your paint job enters the game, you will receive some in-game cash as a token of appreciation. If possible, we'll also give you some car bodies with the paint job on them. You can keep them for your private collection or trade them with other players.

I hope this covers it all. For anyone who is interested in creating skins for Motor City, get ready. When we work out a few logistics internally, we'll open the doors up for submission. And if you have any questions / suggestions, I'll be browsing the Message Boards in the next few days.

Peace.

~~ "Trouble"
