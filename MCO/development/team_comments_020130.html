This is not a feature currently included in the game, but we've seen that it's fairly simple for players to build tournaments and leagues on their own. Right now there are several tournament-style events running on a regular basis, started by clubs and fan-sites. A few of these have been so successful that we asked the organizers to share their insight on how these things should be set up.

Race Tournaments 101:
Tournaments are large, multi-race events. They can be simple or intricate, ranging from quick-and-dirty bracket tourneys (where whomever wins the race makes it to the next round), to more complex tourneys where one must make qualifying times to even be considered for the ladder group, challenges are issued, and racers are divided into skill groups or car classes.

To create your own tournament, you'll first have to figure out what your rules will be. An easy way to start is to draw up a simple bracket structure, like this (say you only want 8 players to start out with):


After designing the structure, figure out what each race's entry fee should be. Remember, only the winners will move on, so they'll get a purse from which to pay subsequent entry fees. Some tournament organizers also suggest charging an overall entry fee for the tournament, which is then distributed as the final prize to the winner. This is optional, of course - the main idea of this kind of tournament is to have a great time racing other people.

How do you judge these races? An easy way we've found is to hop into the race with the two players. Run them as a four-player race with one AI (artificial intelligence) car, and watch the race from the players' perspective (click on the racer's name in your HUD). As the judge you'll finish last, and you can see how they did in the Race Results screen. You can see where it might be nice to have another person working with you to judge these, as it could be a little time-consuming (but we've found it extremely fun, too!).

Now that the planning is done, you need racers! For an event as small as the one we're describing, you'd be wise to individually invite players through the MCO in-game email system. However, there's absolutely nothing wrong with expansion, right? So, where else can you publicize? Try the message boards, your club (if you have one), and even fan-sites that have message boards and chat. When publicizing your tournament you should make sure you cover these 5 basic elements:

  1. Where - Track designation (remember there are level restrictions)
  2. When - Specific date and time for the event)
  3. How - The rules, or where to go to find the rules!
  4. What -
    • Type of race (Sponsored or Open?) and Car Selection limitations (if any)
    • Prize(s) offered
  5. Who -
    • Eligibility requirements (if any)
    • Judge(s) and personas responsible for the Tournament
According to our sources, publicity works best if started 2 weeks prior to the event date. It's a great idea to send an in-game email reminder to entrants weekly, and the day before the event. However, we've seen that small events sometimes do just fine on the "spur of the moment", so if that's your key to enjoyment, please feel free to get out there and start organizing!

If you're not really interested in organizing your own event but would really enjoy participating in one, we encourage you to visit our top fan sites for more information. For example, MCORacing.com has created a set of comprehensive, well-staffed Racing Leagues open to players who are interested in joining for a longer-term playing experience. You may have seen them advertising their popular "Bel Air Cup" series in the MCO message boards. Another great site, "The Untouchables", has tournament events scheduled on their site and will probably be committing to more if interest grows. We'll be providing more information on League racing on the main Motor City Online website in the future.

Joining in a Tournament or League racing experience can be a real thrill, whether you do it as an internal club activity or brave the wild ride of the open tournaments. We know this kind of competition isn't for everyone. But if you're into racing (which most of you are) and are looking for some more excitement and ways to get to know other players, we're sure this will hit the spot!
