  Guideline Provided by MCO Senior Car Artist Skynner:

So you want to create your very own hot rod that every user in the world of MCO will drool over? You want to create a ride that will make all your friends and rivals jealous? There are just a few simple steps to read here that will get you started and then comes the fun part - painting your own custom skin for your favorite car body.

To begin with, we are posting the initial grouping of car skins to the MCO web site that you can download in .TGA format. This is a standard image format that allows us to embed an alpha channel in the image - but you won't need to worry about that! However in order to paint a custom texture you will need any image editor that can read and write in .TGA format.

Before I go into any details, please note that you must abide by the EA.com Terms of Service agreement. Foul language, personal attacks, sexual or racial slurs, and use of copyrighted materials will not be tolerated.

This guideline document is mostly for those of you who are working "blind" by painting the texture without being able to see it on the car model in the game. It will help you save yourselves time by trying to eliminate some of the guesswork involved in how your paint strokes are going to look. However, even if you have a way to put your custom skin in the game (as I know some of you have done already) you might still want to read through this document to get an idea of the considerations we are looking for in custom skins.

Once you've downloaded the TGA files we provide you, go ahead and open them up in your image editor while you follow along in this document.

Getting Started: The first thing you'll want to do is study the texture and familiarize yourself with the layout. Because MCO cars span several decades of style and many different models of American automobiles, there was not one set of rules we could apply for all the cars. And so the decision was made to simply make each car with its own texture layout as opposed to forcing them all to conform to one layout that fits all cars. This CAN happen between some cars, but in many instances each car texture is individual, so study it carefully and find all the relevant landmarks such as the 4 sides of the car chassis (front, rear, side and top), the logos and the other elements that make up the car's skin.

You will also notice that with the exception of detail elements such as the tail-lights, the texture is in grayscale. This is done for a couple of reasons, but what is important for YOU to know is that this allows you to see the car as a set of values from black to white, so that you understand where the shadows and highlights fall on the car body.

Paintable Areas: So what do you paint? Well, uh, wherever the car body is painted. OK, truthfully you can paint all of the texture if your heart desires, but there are certain rules that we have to ask you to follow in order to have your skin approved by us. I won't touch on the abstracts of this, as that is not my area and I am sure will be addressed in every way by those qualified to make those restrictions, but rather I will highlight the technical limitations of the actual texture layout and its details.

Manufacturer Logos: These are important elements, however as we will have to run these through our own processing before they can be put into MCO officially, this is an easy thing for us to replace should the logos be painted over. Ideally, if you have an image editor that supports layers and feel confident you can maintain the legibility of the logos on the car body, by all means paint around them. However in most cases, we will be replacing the logos as part of our own processing of the skin prior to release anyway, so don't worry about painting over them if you want. Be aware that these logos will be put on any final skins that get approved and released, so don't be surprised if your skin is chosen and it isn't 100% the way that you painted it. Sorry, but we can't release a skin without these logos, due to the licenses we hold with the car manufacturers.

Car Details: Along these lines, you MUST also make sure that your artwork doesn't compromise the details of the car. Door, hood and trunk shapes, headlight and tail-light positioning, seam lines of body panels and the like must all be identical to what they are on the original texture. The manufacturer's have approved the look of the MCO cars to reflect factory standards and if you move the tail-lights or change their shape or remove them completely, we can't release your texture. Keep in mind that you are painting the exterior of the car, just like you would in real life. This is not a chance for you to re-invent history and say "What would the '65 Mustang look like with 4 headlights instead of 2?". Any changes to the layout of the car means the submission is disqualified.

Ignore Reference Squares: Don't be square. You may have noticed the presence of some peculiar 8 x 8 pixel squares on the borders of the texture or in unobtrusive places somewhere between the negative space of the sheet. Whatever you do, do NOT paint over them. We use them for our own mystical purposes, so just ignore them and they will ignore you.

There are a few areas of the texture that are not necessary to paint over, however for those of you who want to customize every aspect of your custom skin, read on. Feel free to skip to the last 2 paragraphs of the document if you are only concerned about painting the exterior car body.

Two Dashboards (optional): Paint one dashboard, and simply copy and paste a duplicate next to the first one. There is a reason for having both areas, so don't leave one of the dashboard areas blank.

Interiors (optional): Feel free as well to go hog-wild on your interior upholstery and doors. Just don't change the layout of the interior, as again any changes you make will make it impossible for us to approve your skin.

Car Bottom (optional): We chose to have a generic car bottom for all cars. If you want to change the car bottom of your particular custom skin to be accurate, please do! Just keep it in the same area of the texture at the same size.

Brake Pads (ignore): Just ignore these, or paint them black if you want. It doesn't matter because that space of the texture is not used at all. Or better yet, use that space to sign your work so everyone knows who you are when they download your skin!

A word about image editors. Ideally, if you have the resources, I highly recommend making your custom skin using an application that supports multiple layers, such as Adobe PhotoShop. This will allow you to paint your colors on a separate layer that you can then blend into or simply apply over the original image. If you don't have an image editor that supports layering, you will simply have to exercise a few more of your creative muscles to maintain the quality look of the original texture.

That's about all. You should now be ready to start painting. I won't go into a tutorial on how to use your image editor—I'm sure there are great tutorials out there already and if you have any questions along those lines, your fellow MCO skinners should be able to answer them. I'll also be browsing the MCO Message Boards to help answer some of these questions. I've requested a Custom Paint Jobs folder for the Message Boards. Please look for it in the next few days.

Thanks, and happy skinning!

~~ Skynner
